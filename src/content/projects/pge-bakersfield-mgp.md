## Setting

From 1888 to 1910, a small plant in what is now downtown Bakersfield made gas
for the city's lamps and stoves, first from naphtha and then from coal. It
stood by as a backup into the 1940s, was torn down in 1951, and the lot went on
to a long life as an auto-body shop. What the plant left behind stayed in the
ground: the tars, oils and residues of gas-making.

Sites like it are common. California's utilities built manufactured gas plants
(MGPs) in most towns of any size before natural gas arrived, and PG&E has
identified 43 former MGP sites it owned or operated. Since the 1980s it has been
investigating and cleaning them up under state oversight, and more than 30
have now been remediated. PG&E bought the Bakersfield lot back in 2019 to clean
it up.

## The investigation

Rounds of investigation from 2017 to 2023 put about **60 soil borings** and
**30 soil-vapor probes** into a lot of under an acre, with more than **550
samples** between them. The chemicals of concern are the classic MGP set:
polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) and naphthalene, the most widespread,
along with benzene, petroleum hydrocarbons and lead, and some chlorinated
solvents in soil vapor. Groundwater lies deep here, around 67 feet down, and
was not reached.

The work runs under the **Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC)**:
a voluntary cleanup agreement in 2021, a human health risk assessment and a
**Remedial Action Plan** in 2023, and a CEQA review in 2024. DTSC approved the
plan in December 2024.

## The cleanup

The remedy is excavation. Fieldwork began in July 2026: about **6,400 cubic
yards** of soil dug out to around 6 feet across most of the lot and as deep as
15 feet in one corner, where **shoring** holds the walls, then trucked to a
permitted landfill in about 450 loads and replaced with clean fill.

Digging MGP soil downtown is as much about the neighbors as the ground. The
plan sets **air monitoring** around the perimeter and upwind and downwind,
noise and vibration monitoring, water, foam and plastic sheeting to hold down
dust and odor, tarped trucks on a set route, and confirmation sampling of soil
and soil vapor before the excavation is backfilled.
