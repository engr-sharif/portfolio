## Setting

A modern landfill is built, not dug. Every new cell is a layered engineered
system (subgrade, clay, geomembrane, drainage, protective soil), and every
closed one is sealed under a cover built the same way. Almost none of it can
be seen once the next layer goes on, so each layer is checked as it is built.
That checking is **construction quality assurance (CQA)**: an independent
record, for the owner and the Regional Water Quality Control Board, that what
went into the ground matches the approved design.

My CQA work began at **Yolo County Central Landfill**, the County's regional
landfill a few miles north-east of Davis. It is a site that is always under
construction: about 725 acres, open since 1975, built out in waste management
units and modules; known for its early bioreactor research, including a
U.S. EPA Project XL demonstration; and running its landfill gas through a
County-owned gas-to-energy plant. In those years the County was building new
lined modules, rebuilding lined ponds and impoundments, and closing older
units under final cover.

## What CQA covers

**Earthwork.** Subgrade graded to design and proof-rolled; compacted clay
placed in lifts, each tested for density and moisture before the next goes on.

**Geosynthetics.** Each roll checked against its certifications, panels laid
out to plan, every seam welded and tested (air-pressure tests on
double-wedge seams, destructive samples cut and pulled apart), and every
patch and repair logged and tested.

**Drainage and protection.** Leachate collection pipe and drain rock placed to
grade without damaging the liner below, and the operations layer spread over
it with the equipment kept off bare geomembrane.

**Records.** Daily reports, test results, panel and seam logs, and
nonconformances and how each was resolved: the material that becomes the
CQA report the owner submits to close out the work.

**Landfill gas.** Alongside construction, monitoring the landfill gas system
and perimeter probes.

## Where it led

The same work carried on at other California landfills, each with a page of
its own: independent CQA on the landfill gas system rebuilt beneath the final
cover at [**Colton Sanitary Landfill**](/projects/colton-landfill-final-cover/),
where I wrote the final CQA completion report; and design and CQA on the new
lined cell at [**Woodville Landfill**](/projects/woodville-landfill-cell-expansion/).

## Skills demonstrated

Field CQA on landfill earthwork and geosynthetics; seam testing and
documentation; landfill gas monitoring; reading and enforcing plans and
specifications in the field; and building the construction record that
regulators rely on to accept the work.
