## Setting

Sulphur Bank sits on the Oaks Arm of Clear Lake, California's largest natural
lake. Sulfur was dug here from 1865; mercury from 1873, first underground and,
from 1927, from an open pit, until mining stopped in 1957. The pit then filled
with water and became the **Herman Impoundment**: a pit lake of about 23 acres,
some 90 feet deep, a few hundred feet from Clear Lake's shore. Around it lie
roughly **2.5 to 3 million cubic yards of waste rock, tailings and ore** in nine
piles across a 160-acre mine, including a waste rock dam built out into the lake.

Mercury from the mine has built up in the lake's sediments and fish, and Clear
Lake has carried a fish-consumption advisory since 1987. The site is directly
next to the **Elem Indian Colony**, on land its people have lived on for
thousands of years. EPA listed Sulphur Bank on the National Priorities List in
1990 and ran a series of removal actions over the next two decades, from
regrading the shoreline to cleaning up yards on the Colony.

## The cleanup

EPA's **Record of Decision for the mine (November 2023)** sets out the cleanup:
excavate, consolidate and cap the mine waste; take the waste rock dam back
toward the 1888 shoreline; backfill the Northwest Pit; secure the pit lake and
rebuild the pipelines that divert clean stormwater around it; clean up
residential soils; and keep the whole system monitored and maintained, at an
estimated cost of about $94.5 million. EPA plans the construction in stages
from 2026 into the 2030s, and to use the site's own soil and boulders wherever
it can: about 8,000 fewer truck trips on the roads around the lake.

A remedy like that turns on questions only the ground can answer: how thick the
waste is and where native ground begins, what each pile is made of, and which
material is clean enough to build with. That is what the pre-design
investigation was for.

## My role

- **Pre-design investigation (2025).** I helped lead the **test-pitting** and
  **soil-boring** campaigns, run while the remedial design was being drawn,
  so the field data fed the design as it developed.
- **Boulder characterization.** I helped lead the **handheld XRF campaign**
  that characterized **more than 146 boulders** across the site to support
  their reuse in the remedy.
- **Phase 1 construction.** I lead the **construction effort for Phase 1**
  of the remediation: the cleanup of residential yards next to the mine.

## Test pits and borings

Test pits show the ground in section. An excavator opens a trench through a
waste pile and into what lies under it, and the wall tells the story: roots and
dark soil over weathered rock, bleached and hydrothermally altered material
near the base, the occasional cinnabar-red piece in the spoil. We logged each
profile, sampled it, and screened it with XRF, working inside roped
**exclusion zones**, with **water sprays** holding the dust down and the
excavated material laid out on plastic sheeting before it went back in the
hole.

Borings reach where a trench can't: deeper waste, the contact with native
ground, and material below the water table. Between the two, the campaigns
built a picture of what is where, in the three dimensions a cap design needs.

The design was being drawn in parallel, so turnaround mattered. Data from the
field had to be logged, checked and in the designers' hands quickly enough to
change what they drew next, and field plans shifted as the picture sharpened.

## 146 boulders, one at a time

The mine is strewn with large andesite boulders, and the remedy can use them:
as armoring and riprap, and in place of rock that would otherwise be trucked
in. But a boulder from a mercury mine can only be reused once it has been
shown to be clean enough.

So each boulder was located, tagged and read with **handheld XRF**: readings on
its surface and at depth, with rock chips drilled and powdered for laboratory
analysis to check the field instruments against the lab. Two XRF analyzers
produced thousands of readings, which had to be tied back to the right boulder
without error. I built the [**XRF Data App**](/tools/xrf-data-app/) for exactly
that: it reconciles every reading against the master tracker and shows each
boulder's surface, depth and lab results side by side.

## Phase 1: the yards

The cleanup starts where people live. **Phase 1** is the residential soil
cleanup: yards on the Elem Indian Colony and in the neighborhood along
Sulphur Bank Mine Road, closest to the old workings. EPA's plan is to remove
about 6,000 cubic yards of soil from around a dozen properties, starting in
the fall of 2026, with Tribal Monitors on site alongside the crews.

I lead the construction effort for Phase 1. A residential cleanup is
engineering at the scale of a garden: digging soil out to the depths and
limits in the design, confirming by sampling that what stays behind meets
the residential cleanup levels, bringing in clean soil, and putting each yard
back together. And it is done around people's homes, so dust control,
access, scheduling and keeping residents informed matter as much as the
excavation itself. It is the other half of the pre-design work: the same
ground, now being rebuilt to the design it informed.

## Tools I built for the site

- **The Site Explorer**, in the film above: an in-browser terrain workbench on
  the site's January 2024 aerial survey. It flies the lidar-derived 1-foot
  ground surface in 3D under the same flight's orthophotography, relights the
  hillshade, reads slope and canopy height, and traces where a raindrop would
  run and how flow gathers, in one browser tab with nothing to install. The
  film shows only the terrain; the site's data layers stay out of it.
- [**A field-navigation tool**](/notes/building-a-field-navigation-tool/) that
  walks the crew to each planned sample point on a grid, and shows how close
  they really are.
- [**A sampling planning map**](/tools/soil-sampling-planning-tool/) that puts
  current and historical samples together, finds the gaps, and places new
  points.
- [**The Boring Data Explorer**](/tools/boring-data-explorer/), for reading
  subsurface boring records on a map instead of one file at a time.
- [**The XRF Data App**](/tools/xrf-data-app/), for the boulder campaign.
