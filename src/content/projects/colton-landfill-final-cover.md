## Setting

The Colton Sanitary Landfill is a 113-acre site in the City of Colton, with
88 acres used for waste disposal. It took waste from 1964 until the end of
2014. For final closure, the County of San Bernardino replaced a planned
four-foot soil cover, which would have needed about 600,000 cubic yards of
imported soil, with **ClosureTurf**: a polyethylene geomembrane under a
synthetic-grass layer with sand ballast. According to the County, it is the
largest installation of its kind in Southern California, laid on steep,
highly visible slopes. The County's figures for the closure are about
$39.8 million and 21 months of construction, completed in July 2024.

A new cover changes how gas moves in the waste beneath it, so the closure
included rebuilding the **landfill gas (LFG) collection system**. Public
project summaries describe **171 existing extraction wells abandoned**,
**56 new wells drilled and installed**, and the **headers and laterals
removed and replaced**.

## Our role

The gas system was designed by **Advanced Earth Sciences (AES)**. We were
the **independent third party** providing construction quality assurance
(CQA): confirming for the County that what went into the ground matched the
design, and documenting it.

## What the CQA covered

**Extraction wells.** Each new well starts as a boring drilled down through
the waste. CQA meant being at the rig while the boreholes were drilled, then
watching each well built in it: the casing and screened interval set at the
design depths, the gravel pack placed around the screen, the seal placed
above it, and the wellhead finished. Drilling into waste brings gas, heat
and refuse up to the surface, so every hole is a site-safety task as well as
a construction one.

**Headers and laterals.** The piping that carries gas from each well to the
main headers has to slope as designed so condensate drains instead of
blocking the line. CQA meant checking pipe, grade and connections against
the plans and specifications as they were installed.

**Records.** The CQA record is daily field observations and the as-built
details of what went in. Once the cover is on, those records are the only
way to see the system.

## Skills demonstrated

Construction quality assurance on landfill gas systems; observing drilling
and well construction in waste; field verification against design drawings
and specifications; working as the independent party between the County,
the designer and the contractor; and construction documentation.
